Estimates for a typical 400-hour case · conservative

Most of a case isn't analysis. It's everything around it.

A typical case runs about 400 analyst hours. Only a quarter of that is link charting and analysis. The rest goes into gathering data, cleaning it into shape, rebuilding charts when a source changes, writing the report and reworking it through review.

Linx works on the stages where those hours go. Imports can be re-specified without starting over, charts update when the data does, reports draft themselves from the chart, and every change is kept in the project history. Conservatively, that saves around 100 hours per case.

The built-in AI takes off roughly another 70 hours by drawing connections, answering questions about the data and pointing out what was missed. With 1,800 to 2,000 working hours in a year, that is room for one or two more cases per analyst.

Capabilities

Your data never leaves the building. Not even for us.

Linx runs entirely on your own hardware. One installation includes everything, and every query stays on hardware you control. No cloud platform, no vendor access, and no data pooled with other customers. No API keys, no tokens and no usage meters, so costs don't grow with the work. Nothing leaves the building unless your administrator opens a logged connection.

  • ┌─┐ │▓│ └─┘

    001 / Local computeRuns on your hardware

    Inference happens on the workstation's GPU. No per-token invoices, no usage caps, no queue behind someone else's traffic. The model file sits on disk next to the case, and the thousandth question costs what the first one did: electricity.

  • 002 / ImportStructured and unstructured data import

    Linx handles the full range of data you work with, in whatever file types you have: tables, spreadsheets, notes and reports. You bring it; Linx handles it.

  • 003 / ManageNon-destructive data management

    Map data however you want to visualize it. Change your mind? Update how the data is processed and watch the chart change in real time.

  • 004 / PlotDynamic link analysis charts

    Link charts grow with your data, and visualization tools help make sense of the noise. Organize and filter your data with one click.

  • 005 / EditRobust source control

    Enable and disable sources as needed. Source changes are reflected across the data at every level, and chart elements adjust their sourcing without manual review.

  • 006 / AnalyzeAsk questions of the data

    Analytics help you find connections faster. Ask questions of the data directly, review suggested links and quickly address data that was overlooked.

  • 007 / CollaborateCollaborate across projects

    Data can be shared across projects and brought into your workspace. See suggested connections across all your projects without manually importing project files.

  • 008 / ReviewVersion control and accountability

    Every project keeps a history that follows every change. Audit your work across the project timeline and see how the chart changed. Review goes faster, and no work gets repeated.

  • 009 / ReleaseDissemination made easy

    Generate reports and charts directly from the project. Quickly remove confidential data and produce versions for public release.

Find the gaps

Know what you know. Find what you don't.

Linx flags missing links, unverified connections and blind spots in your analysis, then shows you how to close them. Close the gaps. Every single one.

  • 001 / Sourced links

    Every link comes with its source, so you can see exactly where it came from.

  • 002 / Missing links

    When something doesn't add up, Linx tells you what's missing and where to look next.

  • 003 / Unverified connections

    Connections resting on a single or weak source are marked, so nothing reaches the report unchecked.

  • 004 / People, places, events

    Linx reads your material, finds the people, places and events inside and maps how they connect.

  • 005 / Cross-project suggestions

    See possible connections to your other projects without importing their files.

  • 006 / Defensible output

    Charts and reports trace every conclusion back to the evidence, so the work stands up to review.
